1 Maximum likelihood for a Bernoulli model without covariates

1.1 The likelihood

Exercise 1 (Likelihood of binary outcomes with one event probability) A binary outcome \(Y\) with event probability \(\pi\) has

\[ \begin{aligned} \Pr(Y = 1) &= \pi && \text{(definition of the event probability)}\\ \Pr(Y = 0) &= 1 - \pi && \text{(complement rule)}\\ \Pr(Y = y) &= \pi^y (1 - \pi)^{1 - y}, \quad y \in \mathopen{}\left\{0, 1\right\}\mathclose{} && \text{(combine the two cases)} \end{aligned} \]

Let \(\mathord{\smash[b]{\underset{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}{y}}\vphantom{y}}\stackrel{\text{def}}{=}(y_1, \ldots, y_n)\) be a dataset of mutually independent binary outcomes, all with the same event probability \(\pi\): \(Y_i\ \sim_{\perp\!\!\!\perp}\ \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\). Derive the likelihood of \(\mathord{\smash[b]{\underset{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}{y}}\vphantom{y}}\).

Solution 1. \[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}(\pi; \mathord{\smash[b]{\underset{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}{y}}\vphantom{y}}) &= \prod_{i=1}^n\mathcal{L}_i(\pi) && \text{(product of likelihood components)}\\ &= \prod_{i=1}^n\Pr(Y_i= y_i) && \text{(definition of likelihood components)}\\ &= \prod_{i=1}^n\pi^{y_i} (1 - \pi)^{1 - y_i} && \text{(Bernoulli PMF)}\\ &= \mathopen{}\left(\prod_{i=1}^n\pi^{y_i}\right)\mathclose{} \mathopen{}\left(\prod_{i=1}^n(1 - \pi)^{1 - y_i}\right)\mathclose{} && \text{(regroup the factors of the product)}\\ &= \pi^{\sum_{i=1}^ny_i} (1 - \pi)^{\sum_{i=1}^n(1 - y_i)} && \text{(product of powers of a common base)}\\ &= \pi^{\sum_{i=1}^ny_i} (1 - \pi)^{\sum_{i=1}^n1 - \sum_{i=1}^ny_i} && \text{(split the sum in the exponent)}\\ &= \pi^{\sum_{i=1}^ny_i} (1 - \pi)^{n - \sum_{i=1}^ny_i} && \text{($\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^n1 = n$)} \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 1 (Likelihood of binary outcomes with one event probability) Let \(Y_1, \dots, Y_n\) be mutually independent binary random variables, each with the same event probability \(\pi\): \(Y_i\ \sim_{\perp\!\!\!\perp}\ \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\). For observed outcomes \(\mathord{\smash[b]{\underset{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}{y}}\vphantom{y}}\stackrel{\text{def}}{=}(y_1, \ldots, y_n)\), with each \(y_i\in \mathopen{}\left\{0, 1\right\}\mathclose{}\), the likelihood \(\mathcal{L}(\pi; \mathord{\smash[b]{\underset{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}{y}}\vphantom{y}})\) of \(\pi\) depends on the data only through the number of events \(\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\):

\[\mathcal{L}(\pi; \mathord{\smash[b]{\underset{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}{y}}\vphantom{y}}) = \pi^{\sum_{i=1}^ny_i} (1 - \pi)^{n - \sum_{i=1}^ny_i}\]

Proof. This is the solution to Exercise 1, worked in Solution 1.

1.2 Log-likelihood contributions

Exercise 2 (Log-likelihood contribution of one binary outcome) Let:

  • \(Y_i\ \sim_{\perp\!\!\!\perp}\ \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\) for \(i= 1, \dots, n\), with no covariates;
  • \(y_i\in \mathopen{}\left\{0, 1\right\}\mathclose{}\) be the observed value of \(Y_i\).

Derive the log-likelihood contribution \(\ell_i(\pi)\) of observation \(i\), the log of its likelihood component.

Solution 2. \[ \begin{aligned} \ell_i(\pi) &= {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\Pr(Y_i= y_i)\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(log of the likelihood component)}\\ &= {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi^{y_i} (1 - \pi)^{1 - y_i}\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(Bernoulli PMF)}\\ &= {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi^{y_i}\right\}\mathclose{}} + {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{(1 - \pi)^{1 - y_i}\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(log of a product)}\\ &= y_i{\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi\right\}\mathclose{}} + (1 - y_i) {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(log of a power)} \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 2 (Log-likelihood contribution of one binary outcome) If \(Y_i\sim \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\) has observed value \(y_i\in \mathopen{}\left\{0, 1\right\}\mathclose{}\), its log-likelihood contribution is:

\[\ell_i(\pi) = y_i{\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi\right\}\mathclose{}} + (1 - y_i) {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}}\]

Proof. This is the solution to Exercise 2, worked in Solution 2. Rewriting it with the log-odds \(\operatorname{logit}(\pi)\) and summing over \(i\) gives Theorem 3.

1.3 The log-likelihood of the sample

Exercise 3 (Log-likelihood of binary outcomes with one event probability) Derive the log-likelihood of \(\mathord{\smash[b]{\underset{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}{y}}\vphantom{y}}\) from Exercise 1.

Solution 3. Starting from the likelihood in Theorem 1:

\[ \begin{aligned} \ell(\pi; \mathord{\smash[b]{\underset{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}{y}}\vphantom{y}}) &= {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi^{\sum_{i=1}^ny_i} (1 - \pi)^{n - \sum_{i=1}^ny_i}\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(log of the likelihood)}\\ &= {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi^{\sum_{i=1}^ny_i}\right\}\mathclose{}} + {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{(1 - \pi)^{n - \sum_{i=1}^ny_i}\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(log of a product)}\\ &= \mathopen{}\left(\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\right)\mathclose{} {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi\right\}\mathclose{}} + \mathopen{}\left(n - \sum_{i=1}^ny_i\right)\mathclose{} {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(log of a power)}\\ &= \mathopen{}\left(\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\right)\mathclose{} {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi\right\}\mathclose{}} + n {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}} - \mathopen{}\left(\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\right)\mathclose{} {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(distribute ${\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}}$)}\\ &= \mathopen{}\left(\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\right)\mathclose{} {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi\right\}\mathclose{}} - \mathopen{}\left(\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\right)\mathclose{} {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}} + n {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(reorder the terms)}\\ &= \mathopen{}\left(\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\right)\mathclose{} \mathopen{}\left({\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi\right\}\mathclose{}} - {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}}\right)\mathclose{} + n {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(collect the terms in $\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^ny_i$)}\\ &= \mathopen{}\left(\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\right)\mathclose{} {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\frac{\pi}{1 - \pi}\right\}\mathclose{}} + n {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(log of a quotient)}\\ &= \mathopen{}\left(\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\right)\mathclose{} \operatorname{logit}(\pi) + n {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(definition of $\operatorname{logit}$)} \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 3 (Log-likelihood of binary outcomes with one event probability) Let \(Y_i\ \sim_{\perp\!\!\!\perp}\ \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\) for \(i = 1, \dots, n\), with observed outcomes \(\mathord{\smash[b]{\underset{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}{y}}\vphantom{y}}\stackrel{\text{def}}{=}(y_1, \ldots, y_n)\), each \(y_i\in \mathopen{}\left\{0, 1\right\}\mathclose{}\). The log-likelihood \(\ell(\pi; \mathord{\smash[b]{\underset{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}{y}}\vphantom{y}})\) of the event probability \(\pi\), written with the log-odds \(\operatorname{logit}(\pi)\), is:

\[\ell(\pi; \mathord{\smash[b]{\underset{\raise.3ex\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}{y}}\vphantom{y}}) = \mathopen{}\left(\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\right)\mathclose{} \operatorname{logit}(\pi) + n {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}}\]

Proof. This is the solution to Exercise 3, worked in Solution 3. It builds on Theorem 1.

1.4 The score of one observation

Exercise 4 (Score function of a Bernoulli variable) Derive the score function for a single Bernoulli random variable \(X\). In other words, differentiate the log-likelihood of a single Bernoulli random variable \(X\) with respect to the event probability parameter \(\pi\). Simplify as much as possible.

Solution 4. With \(n = 1\), Theorem 3 gives \(\ell= x {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi\right\}\mathclose{}} + (1 - x) {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}}\), so:

\[ \begin{aligned} \ell' &\stackrel{\text{def}}{=}\frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \ell && \text{(definition of the score)}\\ &= \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \mathopen{}\left(x {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi\right\}\mathclose{}} + (1 - x) {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}}\right)\mathclose{} && \text{(Bernoulli log-likelihood)}\\ &= \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \mathopen{}\left(x {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi\right\}\mathclose{}}\right)\mathclose{} + \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \mathopen{}\left((1 - x) {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}}\right)\mathclose{} && \text{(linearity of differentiation)}\\ &= x \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{\pi\right\}\mathclose{}} + (1 - x) \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(constant multiple rule)}\\ &= x \frac{1}{\pi} + (1 - x) \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} {\operatorname{log}\mathopen{}\left\{1 - \pi\right\}\mathclose{}} && \text{(derivative of $\log$)}\\ &= x \frac{1}{\pi} + (1 - x) \frac{1}{1 - \pi} \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi}(1 - \pi) && \text{(chain rule)} \end{aligned} \]

The inner derivative is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi}(1 - \pi) &= \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} 1 - \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \pi && \text{(linearity of differentiation)}\\ &= 0 - \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \pi && \text{(derivative of a constant)}\\ &= 0 - 1 && \text{(derivative of $\pi$ with respect to itself)}\\ &= -1 && \text{(subtract)} \end{aligned} \]

Substituting the inner derivative back in:

\[ \begin{aligned} \ell' &= x \frac{1}{\pi} + (1 - x) \frac{1}{1 - \pi} (-1) && \text{(inner derivative is $-1$)}\\ &= x \frac{1}{\pi} - (1 - x) \frac{1}{1 - \pi} && \text{(multiply by $-1$)}\\ &= \frac{x}{\pi} - \frac{1 - x}{1 - \pi} && \text{(multiply)}\\ &= \frac{x(1 - \pi)}{\pi(1 - \pi)} - \frac{(1 - x)\pi}{\pi(1 - \pi)} && \text{(write both terms over the common denominator)}\\ &= \frac{x(1 - \pi) - (1 - x)\pi}{\pi(1 - \pi)} && \text{(combine the fractions)}\\ &= \frac{x - x\pi- \pi+ x\pi}{\pi(1 - \pi)} && \text{(expand the numerator)}\\ &= \frac{x - \pi}{\pi(1 - \pi)} && \text{(cancel $x\pi$)}\\ &= \frac{x - {\operatorname{E}\mathopen{}\left[X\right]\mathclose{}}}{\pi(1 - \pi)} && \text{(${\operatorname{E}\mathopen{}\left[X\right]\mathclose{}} = \pi$)}\\ &= \frac{x - {\operatorname{E}\mathopen{}\left[X\right]\mathclose{}}}{{\operatorname{Var}\mathopen{}\left(X\right)\mathclose{}}} && \text{(${\operatorname{Var}\mathopen{}\left(X\right)\mathclose{}} = \pi(1 - \pi)$)} \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 4 (Score function of a Bernoulli variable) Let \(X \sim \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\), with event probability \(\pi\) and observed value \(x \in \mathopen{}\left\{0, 1\right\}\mathclose{}\). The score \(\ell'\) of the single observation \(x\) is the derivative of its log-likelihood \(\ell\) with respect to \(\pi\). It equals the deviation of \(x\) from the mean \({\operatorname{E}\mathopen{}\left[X\right]\mathclose{}}\), divided by the variance \({\operatorname{Var}\mathopen{}\left(X\right)\mathclose{}}\):

\[\ell'= \frac{x - {\operatorname{E}\mathopen{}\left[X\right]\mathclose{}}}{{\operatorname{Var}\mathopen{}\left(X\right)\mathclose{}}} = \frac{x - \pi}{\pi(1 - \pi)}\]

Proof. This is the solution to Exercise 4, worked in Solution 4. It builds on Theorem 3, with \(n = 1\).

1.5 Score contributions

Exercise 5 (Score contribution of one binary outcome) In the setting of Exercise 2, let:

  • \(\tau\stackrel{\text{def}}{=}1 / {\operatorname{Var}\mathopen{}\left(Y_i\right)\mathclose{}}\) be the precision of \(Y_i\).

Derive the score contribution \(\ell'_i(\pi) \stackrel{\text{def}}{=}\frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \ell_i(\pi)\) of observation \(i\), and write it using \(\tau\).

Solution 5. \[ \begin{aligned} \ell'_i(\pi) &= \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \ell_i(\pi) && \text{(definition of the score contribution)}\\ &= \frac{y_i- \pi}{\pi(1 - \pi)} && \text{(score of a Bernoulli variable, with $x = y_i$)}\\ &= \frac{y_i- \pi}{{\operatorname{Var}\mathopen{}\left(Y_i\right)\mathclose{}}} && \text{(${\operatorname{Var}\mathopen{}\left(Y_i\right)\mathclose{}} = \pi(1 - \pi)$)}\\ &= \frac{1}{{\operatorname{Var}\mathopen{}\left(Y_i\right)\mathclose{}}} (y_i- \pi) && \text{(a fraction is its numerator times the reciprocal of its denominator)}\\ &= \tau(y_i- \pi) && \text{(definition of $\tau$)} \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 5 (Score contribution of one binary outcome) If \(Y_i\sim \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\) has observed value \(y_i\in \mathopen{}\left\{0, 1\right\}\mathclose{}\) and precision \(\tau= 1 / {\operatorname{Var}\mathopen{}\left(Y_i\right)\mathclose{}} = 1 / \mathopen{}\left(\pi(1 - \pi)\right)\mathclose{}\), its score contribution is the precision times the deviation of \(y_i\) from its mean \(\pi\):

\[\ell'_i(\pi) = \tau(y_i- \pi)\]

The precision \(\tau\) is the same for every \(i\), because all \(Y_i\) share the event probability \(\pi\).

Proof. This is the solution to Exercise 5, worked in Solution 5.

1.6 The score function of the sample

Exercise 6 (Score function of a binary sample) In the setting of Exercise 5, let \(\bar{y}\stackrel{\text{def}}{=}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\) be the sample mean of the observed outcomes. Assemble the score function \(\ell'(\pi)\) of the whole sample from the score contributions.

Solution 6. \[ \begin{aligned} \ell'(\pi) &= \sum_{i=1}^n\ell'_i(\pi) && \text{(score of an independent sample is the sum of the score contributions)}\\ &= \sum_{i=1}^n\tau(y_i- \pi) && \text{(score contribution of a binary outcome)}\\ &= \tau\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i- \pi) && \text{($\tau$ is the same for every $i$)}\\ &= \tau\mathopen{}\left(\sum_{i=1}^ny_i- \sum_{i=1}^n\pi\right)\mathclose{} && \text{(split the sum)}\\ &= \tau\mathopen{}\left(\sum_{i=1}^ny_i- n \pi\right)\mathclose{} && \text{($\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^n\pi= n \pi$)}\\ &= \tau\mathopen{}\left(n \bar{y}- n \pi\right)\mathclose{} && \text{($\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^ny_i= n \bar{y}$)}\\ &= n \tau(\bar{y}- \pi) && \text{(factor out $n$)} \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 6 (Score function of a binary sample) If \(Y_i\ \sim_{\perp\!\!\!\perp}\ \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\) for \(i= 1, \dots, n\), with observed sample mean \(\bar{y}\) and precision \(\tau= 1 / \mathopen{}\left(\pi(1 - \pi)\right)\mathclose{}\), the score function is:

\[\ell'(\pi) = n \tau(\bar{y}- \pi)\]

Proof. This is the solution to Exercise 6, worked in Solution 6.

1.7 Solving the score equation

Exercise 7 (Solving the score equation of a binary sample) In the setting of Exercise 6, assume \(0 < \bar{y}< 1\): the sample has at least one event and at least one non-event. Solve the score equation \(\ell'(\pi) = 0\) for \(\pi\in (0, 1)\).

Solution 7. For \(\pi\in (0, 1)\), \(\tau= 1 / \mathopen{}\left(\pi(1 - \pi)\right)\mathclose{}\) is finite and positive, and \(n > 0\), so \(n \tau\neq 0\).

\[ \begin{aligned} 0 &= \ell'(\pi) && \text{(score equation)}\\ 0 &= n \tau(\bar{y}- \pi) && \text{(score function of a binary sample)}\\ 0 &= \bar{y}- \pi && \text{(divide both sides by $n \tau\neq 0$)}\\ \pi&= \bar{y} && \text{(add $\pi$ to both sides)} \end{aligned} \]

Since \(0 < \bar{y}< 1\), this solution lies in \((0, 1)\).

Theorem 7 (Solution of the score equation of a binary sample) If \(Y_i\ \sim_{\perp\!\!\!\perp}\ \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\) for \(i= 1, \dots, n\) and the observed sample mean satisfies \(0 < \bar{y}< 1\), then the score equation \(\ell'(\pi) = 0\) has exactly one solution in \((0, 1)\): the sample mean \(\pi= \bar{y}\).

Proof. This is the solution to Exercise 7, worked in Solution 7.

1.8 Hessian contributions

Exercise 8 (Hessian contribution of one binary outcome) In the setting of Exercise 5, derive the Hessian contribution \(\ell''_i(\pi) \stackrel{\text{def}}{=}\frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \ell'_i(\pi)\) of observation \(i\). Start from the score contribution written as a difference of two fractions, \(\ell'_i(\pi) = y_i/ \pi- (1 - y_i) / (1 - \pi)\), the form in Solution 4 before its fractions are combined.

Solution 8. \[ \begin{aligned} \ell''_i(\pi) &= \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \ell'_i(\pi) && \text{(definition of the Hessian contribution)}\\ &= \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \mathopen{}\left(\frac{y_i}{\pi} - \frac{1 - y_i}{1 - \pi}\right)\mathclose{} && \text{(score contribution as a difference of fractions)}\\ &= \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \mathopen{}\left(y_i\pi^{-1} - (1 - y_i) (1 - \pi)^{-1}\right)\mathclose{} && \text{(write each fraction as a negative power)}\\ &= y_i\frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \pi^{-1} - (1 - y_i) \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} (1 - \pi)^{-1} && \text{(linearity and constant multiple rule)} \end{aligned} \]

The first derivative is:

\[ \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} \pi^{-1} = -\pi^{-2} \qquad \text{(power rule)} \]

The second derivative needs the chain rule:

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} (1 - \pi)^{-1} &= -(1 - \pi)^{-2} \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} (1 - \pi) && \text{(chain rule and power rule)}\\ &= -(1 - \pi)^{-2} (-1) && \text{($\frac{\partial}{\partial \pi} (1 - \pi) = -1$, as in the score exercise)}\\ &= (1 - \pi)^{-2} && \text{(multiply)} \end{aligned} \]

Substituting both derivatives back in:

\[ \begin{aligned} \ell''_i(\pi) &= y_i\mathopen{}\left(-\pi^{-2}\right)\mathclose{} - (1 - y_i) (1 - \pi)^{-2} && \text{(substitute the two derivatives)}\\ &= -\frac{y_i}{{\mathopen{}\left(\pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} - \frac{1 - y_i}{{\mathopen{}\left(1 - \pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} && \text{(write the negative powers as fractions)} \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 8 (Hessian contribution of one binary outcome) If \(Y_i\sim \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\) has observed value \(y_i\in \mathopen{}\left\{0, 1\right\}\mathclose{}\), its Hessian contribution is:

\[\ell''_i(\pi) = -\frac{y_i}{{\mathopen{}\left(\pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} - \frac{1 - y_i}{{\mathopen{}\left(1 - \pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}}\]

Proof. This is the solution to Exercise 8, worked in Solution 8.

1.9 The Hessian of the sample

Exercise 9 (Hessian of a binary sample) In the setting of Exercise 6, assemble the Hessian \(\ell''(\pi)\) of the whole sample from the Hessian contributions.

Solution 9. \[ \begin{aligned} \ell''(\pi) &= \sum_{i=1}^n\ell''_i(\pi) && \text{(the derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives)}\\ &= \sum_{i=1}^n\mathopen{}\left(-\frac{y_i}{{\mathopen{}\left(\pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} - \frac{1 - y_i}{{\mathopen{}\left(1 - \pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}}\right)\mathclose{} && \text{(Hessian contribution of a binary outcome)}\\ &= -\frac{1}{{\mathopen{}\left(\pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} \sum_{i=1}^ny_i- \frac{1}{{\mathopen{}\left(1 - \pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} \sum_{i=1}^n(1 - y_i) && \text{(split the sum; the denominators do not depend on $i$)}\\ &= -\frac{n \bar{y}}{{\mathopen{}\left(\pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} - \frac{1}{{\mathopen{}\left(1 - \pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} \mathopen{}\left(n - n \bar{y}\right)\mathclose{} && \text{($\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^ny_i= n \bar{y}$ and $\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^n1 = n$)}\\ &= -\frac{n \bar{y}}{{\mathopen{}\left(\pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} - \frac{n (1 - \bar{y})}{{\mathopen{}\left(1 - \pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} && \text{(factor out $n$)}\\ &= -n \mathopen{}\left(\frac{\bar{y}}{{\mathopen{}\left(\pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} + \frac{1 - \bar{y}}{{\mathopen{}\left(1 - \pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}}\right)\mathclose{} && \text{(factor out $-n$)} \end{aligned} \]

Theorem 9 (Hessian of a binary sample) If \(Y_i\ \sim_{\perp\!\!\!\perp}\ \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\) for \(i= 1, \dots, n\), with observed sample mean \(\bar{y}\), the Hessian of the log-likelihood is the \(1 \times 1\) matrix whose only entry is:

\[\ell''(\pi) = -n \mathopen{}\left(\frac{\bar{y}}{{\mathopen{}\left(\pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} + \frac{1 - \bar{y}}{{\mathopen{}\left(1 - \pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}}\right)\mathclose{}\]

Proof. This is the solution to Exercise 9, worked in Solution 9.

1.10 Checking that the Hessian is negative definite

Exercise 10 (Negative definiteness of the Hessian of a binary sample) In the setting of Exercise 9, show that the Hessian \(\ell''(\pi)\) is negative definite for every \(\pi\in (0, 1)\).

Solution 10. A \(1 \times 1\) matrix is negative definite exactly when its only entry is negative, so it is enough to show \(\ell''(\pi) < 0\).

Fix \(\pi\in (0, 1)\). Then:

  • \({\mathopen{}\left(\pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2} > 0\) and \({\mathopen{}\left(1 - \pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2} > 0\);
  • \(\bar{y}\ge 0\) and \(1 - \bar{y}\ge 0\), because \(y_i\in \mathopen{}\left\{0, 1\right\}\mathclose{}\);
  • \(\bar{y}\) and \(1 - \bar{y}\) are not both zero, because they sum to \(1\).

So \(\bar{y}/ {\mathopen{}\left(\pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2} + (1 - \bar{y}) / {\mathopen{}\left(1 - \pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}\) is a sum of two nonnegative terms, at least one of them positive. It is therefore positive, and with \(n > 0\):

\[ \ell''(\pi) = -n \mathopen{}\left(\frac{\bar{y}}{{\mathopen{}\left(\pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}} + \frac{1 - \bar{y}}{{\mathopen{}\left(1 - \pi\right)\mathclose{}}^{2}}\right)\mathclose{} < 0 \]

This holds for every \(\pi\in (0, 1)\).

Theorem 10 (Negative definiteness of the Hessian of a binary sample) If \(Y_i\ \sim_{\perp\!\!\!\perp}\ \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\) for \(i= 1, \dots, n\), the Hessian \(\ell''(\pi)\) of the log-likelihood is negative definite for every \(\pi\in (0, 1)\), whatever the observed outcomes. Hence the log-likelihood is strictly concave on \((0, 1)\).

Proof. This is the solution to Exercise 10, worked in Solution 10. It builds on Theorem 9.

1.11 The maximum likelihood estimate

Exercise 11 (Maximum likelihood estimate of a binary sample) In the setting of Exercise 7, use Theorem 10 to find the maximum likelihood estimate \(\hat{\pi}_{\text{ML}}\) of \(\pi\).

Solution 11. By Theorem 10, \(\ell\) is strictly concave on \((0, 1)\). A strictly concave function that has a critical point is maximized there, and nowhere else. With \(0 < \bar{y}< 1\), Theorem 7 says the only critical point of \(\ell\) in \((0, 1)\) is \(\bar{y}\). So \(\ell\) is maximized at \(\pi= \bar{y}\).

Theorem 11 (Maximum likelihood estimate of a binary sample) If \(Y_i\ \sim_{\perp\!\!\!\perp}\ \operatorname{Ber}(\pi)\) for \(i= 1, \dots, n\) and the observed sample mean satisfies \(0 < \bar{y}< 1\), the maximum likelihood estimate of \(\pi\) is the sample mean:

\[\hat{\pi}_{\text{ML}} = \bar{y}\]

Proof. This is the solution to Exercise 11, worked in Solution 11. It builds on Theorem 7 and Theorem 10.
